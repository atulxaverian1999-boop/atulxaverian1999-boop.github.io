ITR Guide

Which ITR form should you file?

Filing the wrong form leads to a "defective return" notice. Answer three questions to find yours, then read the full guide for every form — ITR-1 to ITR-7.

ITR-1 Sahaj — salaried & pensioners

✅ Who can file

  • Resident individual (ordinarily resident)
  • Total income up to ₹50 lakh
  • Salary or pension
  • Income from up to two house properties
  • Interest, dividend, family pension (other sources)
  • Long-term capital gains on listed shares / equity MF up to ₹1.25 lakh
  • Agricultural income up to ₹5,000

❌ Who cannot

  • Total income above ₹50 lakh
  • Any business or professional income
  • Short-term capital gains, or other capital gains
  • Director in a company, or holding unlisted shares
  • Foreign assets, foreign income or foreign tax credit
  • NRIs and "resident but not ordinarily resident"
  • Lottery / race-horse winnings; losses to carry forward
  • Deferred tax on ESOPs from a start-up

What the form asks

Personal details, salary break-up (from Form 16), exempt allowances, house property, other income, deductions, TDS/TCS and taxes paid, and bank accounts. Most of it is pre-filled from AIS / Form 26AS — check every figure.

Keep ready

Form 16, AIS & Form 26AS, interest certificates, rent and home-loan interest certificates, deduction proofs.

ITR-2 Investors, high income, NRIs

✅ Who can file

  • Individuals and HUFs with no business / professional income
  • Capital gains of any kind — shares, MF, property, gold
  • Total income above ₹50 lakh
  • More than two house properties
  • Foreign assets or income; NRIs and RNORs
  • Directors, holders of unlisted shares
  • Agricultural income above ₹5,000
  • Clubbed income of spouse / minor child
  • Losses to carry forward or set off

❌ Who cannot

  • Business or professional income
  • Interest, salary, bonus or commission from a partnership firm (use ITR-3)

Key schedules

Schedule CG (asset-wise capital gains, with dates, cost, indexation for pre-23 July 2024 land/buildings and exemptions u/s 54 / 54F / 54EC), Schedule 112A (scrip-wise equity LTCG with 31-Jan-2018 values), Schedule FA (foreign assets — mandatory for residents holding any), Schedule AL (assets & liabilities if income exceeds ₹1 crore), Schedule CFL (losses carried forward), Schedule SI (special-rate income).

Keep ready

Broker capital-gains statement, CAMS / KFintech statement, property sale and purchase deeds, foreign account statements, previous years' loss details.

ITR-3 Business & profession (regular)

✅ Who can file

  • Individuals and HUFs with income from business or profession
  • Professionals maintaining books (doctors, consultants, freelancers not on presumptive basis)
  • F&O and intraday traders
  • Partners receiving salary, interest or share from a firm
  • Anyone who would file ITR-2 but also has business income

❌ Who cannot

  • Firms, LLPs and companies (use ITR-5 / ITR-6)

Key schedules

Balance sheet & P&L (or "No account case" details), Schedule BP (computation of business income with disallowances), depreciation schedules, Schedule OI (other information — method of accounting, ICDS effect), turnover from F&O and intraday, tax audit details if applicable, plus all schedules of ITR-2.

Keep ready

Books of accounts, bank statements, GST returns, broker P&L for F&O, fixed-asset register, loan statements, audit report (if audited).

ITR-4 Sugam — presumptive income

✅ Who can file

  • Resident individuals, HUFs and firms (other than LLPs)
  • Total income up to ₹50 lakh
  • Business income on presumptive basis — 6% / 8% of turnover
  • Professional income — 50% of receipts
  • Goods carriages — fixed income per vehicle
  • Salary, house property, interest; equity LTCG up to ₹1.25 lakh

❌ Who cannot

  • Total income above ₹50 lakh
  • Directors, unlisted shareholders, foreign assets / income
  • Capital gains other than small equity LTCG
  • Those keeping regular books and declaring lower profit (use ITR-3)
  • Losses to carry forward

What the form asks

Gross turnover / receipts split by digital and cash, presumptive income, and basic financial particulars — sundry debtors, creditors, stock, cash balance. No full balance sheet needed.

Watch out

If you opt out of presumptive taxation, you may not be able to opt back in for the next five years, and a tax audit may apply.

ITR-5 Firms, LLPs, AOPs, BOIs

Who files it

Partnership firms, LLPs, associations of persons (AOP), bodies of individuals (BOI), artificial juridical persons, local authorities, co-operative societies, business trusts and investment funds — any person other than individuals, HUFs, companies and those filing ITR-7.

Key schedules

Partners' / members' details and profit-sharing ratio, balance sheet and P&L, Schedule BP, depreciation, capital gains, interest and remuneration to partners within limits, tax audit details.

ITR-6 Companies

Who files it

All companies — private, public, one-person and foreign companies — other than companies claiming exemption as charitable or religious institutions (they file ITR-7).

Key schedules

Financial statements in Schedule III format (Ind AS or AS), shareholding details, Schedule BP, depreciation, MAT computation where applicable, concessional-rate regimes for domestic companies, CSR spend, related-party transactions, and audit report details.

Must know

Companies must file electronically under digital signature. Tax audit report is filed before the return.

ITR-7 Trusts, institutions, political parties

Who files it

Charitable and religious trusts, political parties, scientific research associations, news agencies, universities, hospitals and other institutions, and other bodies required to file returns as exempt or specified entities.

Key schedules

Registration details, application of income for charitable purposes, accumulation, voluntary contributions and corpus donations, anonymous donations, investments in specified modes, and audit report details.

⏰ Missed the due date?

A belated return can be filed by 31 December after the year ends, with a late fee of up to ₹5,000 (₹1,000 if income is up to ₹5 lakh).

✏️ Made a mistake?

File a revised return. From the Finance Act 2026, revision is allowed up to 12 months from the end of the tax year (a fee applies after 9 months).

🔁 Forgot some income?

An updated return (ITR-U) can be filed within 4 years of the end of the assessment year, by paying additional tax of 25%–70% depending on the delay.

✔️ Don't forget to verify

A return is complete only after e-verification — through Aadhaar OTP, net banking or a demat/bank account — within 30 days of filing.

For income earned in FY 2026-27 (Tax Year 2026-27), return forms will be notified under the Income-tax Act, 2025. Their structure is expected to follow the forms above; this guide will be updated when they are notified.

What's New

Latest changes & what they mean for you

Each update shows the date and what changed. Tap + for the full explanation with an example.

Oct2025
PF / EPFO

PF partial withdrawal rules simplified — 75% can be withdrawn, 25% must stay

What changed: EPFO’s trustee board merged 13 withdrawal reasons into 3 categories. You can now withdraw up to 75% of your PF balance (including the employer’s share), but 25% must always remain. Roll-out to members continues in 2026.

The new structure

CategoryCoversHow often
Essential needsIllness, education, marriageIllness — no limit · Education — up to 10 times · Marriage — up to 5 times
HousingBuying / building / repaying a home loanUp to 5 times
Special circumstancesNatural calamity, lockout, unemployment etc. — no reason needs to be provedUp to 2 times a year

Key rules

  • Withdraw up to 75% of the eligible balance — employee share, employer share and interest
  • 25% of the balance must stay in the account at all times (it keeps earning interest)
  • Minimum 12 months of membership for every advance
  • Lost your job? 75% can be withdrawn immediately; the rest after 12 months of unemployment (earlier: full withdrawal after 2 months)
  • Pension (EPS) final withdrawal after 36 months of unemployment (earlier: 2 months)

💡 Example

Riya’s PF balance is ₹8,00,000 (her share ₹4.6 lakh, employer’s ₹3.4 lakh incl. interest). She needs money for her sister’s wedding. Earlier she could withdraw only up to 50% of her own share — about ₹2.3 lakh. Now she can withdraw up to ₹6,00,000 (75% of the whole balance). ₹2,00,000 stays invested.

📌 Impact for you

  • Much easier to access your money in a genuine need — fewer claim rejections
  • But retirement savings fall faster — the 25% floor is a safety net, not a target
  • Tax: partial advances are generally not taxed. A full withdrawal before 5 years of continuous service is taxable, and TDS of 10% applies if it exceeds ₹50,000 (s. 392 of the new Act, old 192A)

Sources: EPF new withdrawal rules (Oct 2025) · Roll-out details (Sep 2026) · confirm the latest on epfindia.gov.in

Jun2026
PF / EPFO

EPF interest for FY 2025-26 stays at 8.25%

What changed: The rate decided by EPFO’s board on 2 March 2026 was approved by the Finance Ministry in June 2026 — 8.25% for the third year running.

💡 Example

Average PF balance through the year ₹5,00,000 → interest ≈ ₹41,250 credited for FY 2025-26.

📌 Tax angle

  • PF interest is tax-free, except interest on your own contributions above ₹2.5 lakh a year (₹5 lakh if your employer doesn’t contribute). That part is taxable as “income from other sources” and TDS may apply.
  • Employer contributions to PF + NPS + superannuation above ₹7.5 lakh a year are taxable as a perquisite.

Source: EPF interest retained at 8.25% for FY26

Nov2025
Labour codes

New labour codes notified — “wages” must be at least 50% of pay

What changed: The four labour codes were notified on 21 November 2025. If allowances exceed 50% of total pay, the excess is added back to “wages” — the base for PF, gratuity and other benefits. The PF wage ceiling stays at ₹15,000 a month (notification of 29 May 2026).

💡 Example

Monthly pay ₹60,000 = basic ₹20,000 + allowances ₹40,000. Allowances are 67% of pay, so ₹10,000 is added back — “wages” become ₹30,000.
PF: if your employer contributes on the ₹15,000 ceiling, PF stays ₹1,800 and take-home doesn’t change (as clarified by the Labour Ministry). If PF is on actual wages, it rises from ₹2,400 to ₹3,600 a month — lower take-home, higher savings.
Gratuity: calculated on the higher wage base — a bigger payout when you leave.

📌 Impact for you

  • Expect salary restructuring — higher basic, lower allowances
  • Old-regime HRA exemption is linked to basic + DA, so a higher basic can increase HRA exemption
  • Employers’ PF and gratuity costs rise; check your new salary break-up

Sources: Labour Ministry clarification · Wage ceiling ₹15,000 retained

Jul2026
Income tax

Cost Inflation Index for FY 2026-27 notified at 384

What changed: CBDT notified CII of 384 (FY 2025-26: 376). It is used for the 20%-with-indexation option on land / buildings bought before 23 July 2024.

💡 Example

Flat bought in FY 2010-11 (CII 167) for ₹30 lakh, sold in FY 2026-27 for ₹90 lakh. Indexed cost = 30 × 384 / 167 = ₹68.98 lakh → gain ₹21.02 lakh → tax at 20% ≈ ₹4.2 lakh, versus 12.5% on ₹60 lakh = ₹7.5 lakh without indexation. You pay the lower one. Try it in the Advance Calculator → Detailed computation.

Source: CII 384 notified

Apr2026
Income tax

Budget 2026 changes now in force

What changed: Buyback taxed as capital gains, higher STT on F&O, lower TCS on education / medical remittances and tour packages, 31 August due date for non-audit business cases, longer window for revised returns.

  • Share buyback — taxed as capital gains in the shareholder’s hands, not as dividend
  • STT on F&O — futures 0.05%, options 0.15%
  • TCS — education / medical remittances above ₹10 lakh: 2%; overseas tour packages: flat 2%
  • ITR due date — non-audit business / professional cases: 31 August (ITR-1 / ITR-2 stay 31 July)
  • Revised return — up to 12 months from the end of the tax year (fee after 9 months)
  • Interest to earn dividend / MF income — no longer deductible

Source: Finance Bill 2026 summary · read the details in the Tax Library

Apr2026
Income tax

Income-tax Act, 2025 replaces the 1961 Act

What changed: From 1 April 2026 the new Act applies — 536 sections, “Tax Year” instead of previous / assessment year, all TDS sections merged into s. 393. Rates are unchanged. HRA 50% limit now covers 8 cities.

Income of FY 2025-26 is still filed under the 1961 Act (AY 2026-27). From Tax Year 2026-27, section numbers change — for example 80C → s. 123, 87A → s. 156, 194A → s. 393. Search any old number in the Tax Library to find its new home.

    The Income-tax Act, 2025 is now in force

    From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Act, 1961 has been replaced. Here's what it means for your returns.

    Which law applies to my return?

    Income earned inFiled inLaw
    FY 2025-262026 (AY 2026-27)Income-tax Act, 1961
    Tax Year 2026-272027Income-tax Act, 2025

    What changes

    "Tax Year" replaces Previous Year / Assessment Year. Income earned from April 2026 to March 2027 is simply Tax Year 2026-27.

    Section numbers are renumbered. For example, the new tax regime (old Sec 115BAC) is now Section 202.

    Tax rates and slabs are unchanged for Tax Year 2026-27 — the new regime, ₹12 lakh rebate (now s. 156) and ₹75,000 standard deduction continue.

    New for Tax Year 2026-27 (Budget 2026)

    • HRA 50% limit extended to Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad
    • Share buyback proceeds taxed as capital gains, not dividend
    • Higher STT on futures and options
    • TCS on overseas tour packages flat 2%; LRS for education / medical 2%
    • ITR due date for non-audit business / professional cases moves to 31 August
    • Revised return allowed up to 12 months from end of the tax year (fee after 9 months)
    • Interest paid to earn dividend / MF income no longer deductible
    • Key renumbering: 80C → s. 123, 80D → s. 126, 111A → s. 196, 112A → s. 198, TDS → s. 393

    FAQ

    Common income tax questions

    Should I choose the old or the new tax regime?

    It depends on your deductions. The new regime has lower rates and a larger rebate — for most resident individuals there's no tax on income up to ₹12 lakh (₹12.75 lakh for salaried, after the standard deduction). The old regime can work out better if you have large HRA, 80C, 80D and home-loan interest claims. Run both through the calculator.

    Does the new Income-tax Act, 2025 change my return this year?

    No. Income earned in FY 2025-26 is filed under the Income-tax Act, 1961 (AY 2026-27). The 2025 Act applies from Tax Year 2026-27, whose return is filed in 2027.

    I missed the due date. Can I still file?

    You can usually file a belated return within the time allowed after the due date, with a late fee. Beyond that, an updated return (ITR-U) may be possible with additional tax, subject to conditions.

    What are AIS and Form 26AS?

    They are statements on the income-tax e-filing portal showing TDS, interest, dividends and high-value transactions reported against your PAN. Check your return against them before filing to avoid mismatch notices.

    Should I share my income-tax portal password with someone filing for me?

    No. Share the documents they need, or use the portal's own process to authorise a representative. Your password should stay with you.
